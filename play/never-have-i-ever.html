What the game is

Players. Three people or more, best from five

Works best. For a table that has already warmed up and is ready to admit things

Someone reads a card out loud and everyone who has done it owns up. That part takes five seconds. The good part comes next, because every confession has a story behind it and the table always asks for it.

We wrote the cards so that the answer leaves a hole you want to poke. That is why you will not find "never have I ever been abroad" in the set. Small, recognisable, slightly embarrassing things work far better than big ones.

Rules

  1. Read the card out loud, exactly as written, without softening it
  2. Everyone who has done it owns up, a raised hand counts
  3. Whoever admitted it first tells the table how it happened
  4. The next person clockwise reads the next card
  5. Anyone can pass once, and nobody gets talked into anything

Cards from the set

  • Never have I ever answered a text three weeks late and opened with "sorry, just saw this"
  • Never have I ever pretended to know a song and mumbled the whole chorus
  • Never have I ever left a group chat and immediately regretted it
  • Never have I ever taken a longer route home just to finish a podcast
  • Never have I ever agreed to plans I was already planning to cancel
  • Never have I ever rehearsed a phone call before making it

Ways to play

Without drinking

Count points instead of sips. One confession, one point. By the end of the night the person with the most points has clearly lived the most, and that is funny on its own.

For two

Read the cards in turn and both answer every one. With two people it stops being a game and turns into a proper conversation, which is usually better.

For a big group

From eight people, split the table in half and read each card for both halves at once. Nobody sits waiting ten minutes for a turn.

Over a video call

One person keeps the cards and reads them, everyone else answers in gallery order. Otherwise four people talk at the same time and you hear none of them.

How to run it well

  • Do not explain the card before reading it. Half the laugh is in how differently everyone reads the same line
  • If the whole table owns up, ask who had it worst instead of who did it
  • Leave the quiet one alone. Two cards later they usually start talking on their own

Questions about this game

How many people do you need?

Three is enough, five or more is better. The more people at the table, the more often someone owns up, and the confession is what starts the stories.

Does it need drinking?

No. The set is written so it works over tea. Count points for confessions, or skip scoring and just tell the stories.

Will it work with people who barely know each other?

Start with the harmless cards and move to personal ones once people stop glancing at each other. The web deck opens with the softer questions for that reason.

Can I play in the browser?

Yes, right on this page. Eight cards a day are free here. The app holds a far bigger set and remembers what you have already asked.
