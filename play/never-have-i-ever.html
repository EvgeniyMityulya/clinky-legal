Play Never Have I Ever online

Never Have I Ever is a party game where someone reads a confession out loud and everyone who has done it owns up. You can play it free in the browser on one phone passed around the table, without signing up or installing anything.

8 free cards a day, refreshed daily.

What is this game?

How many players? 3+ players

When does it land? A warmed-up table, once people stopped being polite

Someone reads a card out loud and everyone who has done it owns up. That takes five seconds, and then the good part starts, because every confession has a story behind it and the table always asks for it. The cards are written for exactly that, which is why the set has no "never have I ever been abroad" in it.

Rules

  1. Read the card out loud, exactly as written
  2. Everyone who has done it owns up, a raised hand counts
  3. Whoever admitted it first tells the table how it happened
  4. The next person clockwise takes the following card
  5. Anyone can pass once and nobody gets talked into it

How else can you play?

  1. Without drinking, count points instead of sips, and by the end it is clear who has lived the most
  2. For two, read in turns and both answer, which turns the game into a long honest conversation
  3. From eight people, split the table in half so nobody waits ten minutes for their turn
  4. On a video call, one person reads the cards and everyone else answers in gallery order

Advice for the host

  1. Do not explain the card, half the laugh is in how differently people read the same line
  2. If everyone owns up, ask who had it worse rather than who did it at all
  3. Leave the quiet one alone, two cards later they usually start talking on their own

Questions about this game

How many people do you need?

Three is enough and five or more is better. The more people at the table, the more often someone owns up, and the confession is what starts the stories.

Does it need drinking?

No. The set works over tea just as well. Count points for confessions, or skip the scoring and simply tell the stories.

Will it work with people who barely know each other?

Start with the harmless cards and move to personal ones once people stop glancing at each other. The web deck opens with the softer questions for that reason.

Can I play in the browser?

Yes, right on this page. Eight cards a day are free here, and the app holds a far bigger set that remembers what you have already asked.
